<div class="answer" markdown="1">
Satellite user terminals that transmit (phones, messengers, IoT trackers, VSAT and in-motion terminals) are certified in Canada under RSS-170 and related standards for mobile-satellite and fixed-satellite earth stations, and the Canadian Representative requirement applies to foreign applicants in the usual way. Receive-only GNSS receivers are not transmitters and need no certification. Operation of the terminal on a satellite network is a separate licensing matter for the operator.
</div>

## What is specific to this category

**Certification versus licensing.** ISED certifies the terminal as radio apparatus under RSP-100 and the applicable RSS (RSS-170 for mobile earth stations in several bands; RSS-192 and RSS-195 for fixed wireless and related services; other standards by band). Whether the terminal may be operated in Canada on a given satellite network is a spectrum-licensing question for the network operator, governed by the operator's Canadian authorization and, for some services, by licence-exempt status. The Canadian Representative role belongs to the certification side only.

**Combined terminals.** Many satellite messengers and IoT devices also contain Bluetooth or Wi-Fi (RSS-247) and cellular (RSS-130 family). Separate certifications mean separate letters. See [one representative letter per IC ID](/guides/one-representative-letter-per-ic-id/).

**RF exposure.** Handheld satellite phones and messengers transmit at higher power than terrestrial consumer devices, and RSS-102 SAR evaluation is the norm. Fixed and vehicle-mounted terminals are evaluated for MPE at the relevant distance.

**In-motion terminals.** Earth stations in motion (aircraft, maritime, vehicular) operating in Ku and Ka bands are subject to specific standards and operator authorization. The certification path and representative requirement are unchanged.

**Foreign applicants are the rule.** Satellite terminal makers are concentrated in the United States, Europe and Israel. Nearly every applicant in this category needs a Canadian Representative under RSP-100 section 4.1. See [the requirement explained](/guides/canadian-representative-requirement-rsp-100/).

**Long service.** Maritime and remote-monitoring terminals stay in service for many years. Choose a representative that will be there. See [how long must a Canadian Representative be appointed](/guides/how-long-must-a-canadian-representative-be-appointed/).

## Typical certification path

Lab tests to RSS-170 or the applicable satellite RSS, plus RSS-247 and RSS-130 family for any terrestrial radios, RSS-102 exposure, RSS-Gen. CB issues the IC number. The applicant, if outside Canada, orders the Canadian Representative letter at application; we issue the same business day. Network authorization proceeds separately with the satellite operator.
