Chinese manufacturers are the largest group of applicants needing a Canadian Representative under RSP-100 section 4.1. Testing is normally done at a recognized lab in Shenzhen, Shanghai, Dongguan or Suzhou; certification goes to a Canadian CB or a recognized FCB elsewhere, because ISED does not recognize certification bodies in mainland China. Order overnight China time, pay by card in USD, and the letter is normally issued before your next working day begins.
</div>

## How certification usually runs from China

ISED recognizes many testing laboratories in China for RSS testing, so test data is produced locally. The certification decision, however, is made by a certification body ISED recognizes, and mainland China is not party to a mutual recognition arrangement with Canada for certification bodies. In practice the lab submits the file to a Canadian CB or to a recognized FCB in the United States, Taiwan, Hong Kong or Europe. The CB asks for the Canadian Representative details as part of the application. Check ISED's current list of recognized FCBs if your lab proposes a CB you have not used before.

## What we see from Chinese applicants

**Legal name in English.** The applicant name on the letter must match the English legal name used on the application and the REL listing, and that should match the name on the business licence translation your lab uses. "Shenzhen XYZ Technology Co., Ltd." in one place and "Shenzhen XYZ Tech Ltd" in another causes CB returns. Send us the exact string your lab is using. See [attestation letter required fields](/guides/attestation-letter-required-fields/).

**Factory versus applicant.** The applicant is often a Hong Kong or BVI holding company while the factory is in Guangdong. The representative letter names the applicant. If the applicant later changes to the factory entity, that is a certificate transfer, not a rename; see [changing your Canadian Representative](/guides/changing-your-canadian-representative/).

**Labs ordering for clients.** Many Chinese labs order the letter on the client's behalf as part of a turnkey FCC/ISED package. We issue the letter naming the client as applicant; the lab can take an [annual plan](/pricing/) and bill its clients.

**ODM and brand.** Where a Shenzhen ODM certifies in its own name and a Western brand sells under its label, the ODM is the applicant and needs the representative. Where the brand certifies in its own name, the brand does. One of the two, not both.

**Modules.** Most Chinese consumer devices use certified Wi-Fi/BLE modules from Chinese module makers. The module maker needs the representative for the module; the device maker needs one only for certifications it holds. See [one representative letter per IC ID](/guides/one-representative-letter-per-ic-id/).

## Payment and timing

Card payment through Stripe in USD works with UnionPay-co-branded Visa and Mastercard and with most corporate cards; it is the fastest route. Wire transfers in USD are accepted and typically arrive in two to three banking days. We invoice in USD; a formal invoice (形式发票) is available for your finance department. Orders submitted in the Chinese afternoon or evening are processed during the Ottawa business day and the letter is usually in your inbox when you start the next morning. We correspond in English; Chinese-language enquiries are welcome and we reply in English.

## Audit samples from China

If ISED requests a sample, you ship from production stock to ISED's laboratory in Canada, as a regulatory sample, at your cost. We coordinate and can advise on the Canadian customs paperwork; see [ISED audit samples](/guides/ised-audit-samples-who-provides-who-pays/).
