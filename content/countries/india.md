Indian manufacturers need a Canadian Representative under RSP-100 section 4.1 like any applicant outside Canada. India has no mutual recognition arrangement with Canada for certification bodies, so the certificate is issued by a Canadian CB or by a recognized FCB elsewhere, usually working from a recognized Indian or overseas lab. Payment and remittance paperwork is the practical issue for Indian applicants, and we have it covered.
</div>

## How certification usually runs from India

Several Indian laboratories hold ISED recognition for RSS testing; others subcontract to labs in Taiwan or the US. Certification is by a Canadian CB or a recognized FCB in the United States, Taiwan or Europe. The CB requests the Canadian Representative's details and letter at application. Indian regulatory teams familiar with WPC ETA and BIS processes will find the ISED representative requirement simpler than either, but it is mandatory.

## What we see from Indian applicants

**IoT, telecom equipment and modules.** Indian applicants skew to IoT devices, telecom and networking equipment, and increasingly to domestically designed modules and wearables. Telecom equipment may also be terminal equipment under DC-01, which carries the equivalent Canadian contact requirement; tell us which applies.

**Entity naming.** "Private Limited" as registered, consistent with the CB's application. Group structures with a US or Singapore sales entity as applicant are common; the applicant entity needs the representative.

**Payment paperwork.** Outward remittance from India requires an invoice and, typically, Form 15CA/CB for the remitting bank. We provide a USD invoice and any supplier declaration your bank requires. Card payment through Stripe avoids the remittance process entirely for the per-certification fee and is the route most Indian clients take.

**Procurement documentation.** Vendor registration forms and a pro-forma invoice are provided on request.

## Payment and timing

Card via Stripe in USD is immediate and avoids remittance paperwork. Wires are accepted and typically arrive in two to four banking days after the remittance is approved. Orders placed during the Indian working day are processed the same Ottawa business day and the letter is normally waiting the next morning in India.
