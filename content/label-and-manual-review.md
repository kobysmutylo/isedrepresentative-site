## Why a label review is worth doing before you print

Many Canadian labelling gaps start with FCC artwork carried over unchanged: an FCC ID and no IC number, a manual without the French notices, an HVIN that doesn't match the REL, or a QR code that links to a web page. The certification body may catch some of these, and the rest can surface after launch, when units are already in stock. A label that doesn't match the certification is one of the gaps covered in [the consequences of ISED non-compliance](/guides/ised-non-compliance-penalties/).

## What we check

**The radio label (RSS-Gen section 9.4).**

- The certification number is present and preceded by "IC:". The company number and product number are in the right format, and only digits and capital letters are used.
- The HVIN is present, the PMN is on the label, packaging or literature, and all three match the REL listing exactly. We check for wildcard characters.
- Host products: whether you need "Contains IC:" for each module, and the host marketing name. We also check that the host label follows the module maker's integration instructions.
- The labelling method: a permanent label, a QR code that meets the four section 9.4.2 conditions, or the small-device rule. If you use an e-label, we check it against Annex B, including the rules for devices without a screen.

**The user manual (RSS-Gen section 9.3).**

- Every required notice appears in English and in French. That includes the licence-exempt notice and, where there is a detachable antenna, the antenna notice with types and maximum gain.
- The RF exposure statement your test lab specified under RSS-102 appears in both languages.
- If the manual is online, each unit ships with instructions for finding it, and your plan keeps it available for as long as the product is sold in Canada.

**The ICES-003 marking.** Where the product also carries the [CAN ICES-003(B)/NMB-003(B)](/guides/can-ices-003-b-nmb-003-b-label/) marking, we check that its form and placement fit the ICES-Gen requirements. A label combining both markings has to satisfy both standards.

**Packaging.** Wherever RSS-Gen requires information on the packaging, we check that it's on the outside of the box. We also point out anything that seems to raise a question under Canada's consumer-packaging or Quebec French-language rules. Those regimes are separate from ISED's, so treat our note as a pointer, not a review of them.

## What you get

## What it does not cover

We do not test or inspect the product. We do not decide which RSS standards apply to it, evaluate RF exposure, or give an opinion on technical compliance. We also do not translate. We check that the French notices are present and match the required wording, and your translator handles the rest. Your certification body has the final word on the label for a specific device, and our comments don't replace that. The full terms are in [section 1A of the terms of service](/terms/).
