RSP-100 section 4.1 requires every applicant for ISED radio equipment certification whose company address is outside Canada to identify a Canadian Representative on its application. The representative must respond to all ISED enquiries about the certified product and provide audit samples to ISED at no charge, and the arrangement must stay in place for as long as the product is offered in Canada.
</div>

## The text

RSP-100 is the Radio Standards Procedure that governs certification of radio apparatus and broadcasting equipment in Canada. Section 4.1 reads, in full:

> "A Canadian representative is required when the applicant's company address is not within Canada. The applicant shall provide a signed Application and Agreement for Certification Services (see form A) in which the details of the company representative in Canada shall be clearly identified. The Canadian representative shall be responsible for responding to all enquiries from ISED regarding the certified product(s), including providing audit samples at no charge to ISED. The applicant shall have a valid Application and Agreement for Certification Services for as long as the certified product is offered on the Canadian market."

## Four things the section establishes

**The trigger is the applicant's address.** Not the brand owner's, not the factory's, not the importer's. If the company named as applicant on the certification application has an address outside Canada, a representative is required. A Canadian subsidiary that is the applicant, with a real Canadian place of business, satisfies the section without a separate representative; a virtual office does not, because the representative must be able to receive enquiries and produce samples.

**The representative is identified on the certification application itself.** The "Application and Agreement for Certification Services" is the CB's application form (form A in RSP-100). The representative's details go on it, which is why certification bodies ask for the representative's letter before they will issue: the CB is attesting to ISED that a representative exists.

**The representative's duties are specific.** Respond to ISED enquiries about the certified product, and make audit samples available at no charge to ISED. The section does not make the representative responsible for the product's compliance, for testing, for labelling, or for paying for samples; RSP-100 section 12.2 places the cost of audit samples on the certificate holder. See [ISED audit samples: who provides, who pays](/guides/ised-audit-samples-who-provides-who-pays/).

**The obligation is continuous.** "For as long as the certified product is offered on the Canadian market." The representative is not a one-time signature for the application; it is the Canadian contact for the commercial life of the product. This is the point most often misunderstood, and it drives everything about how a representative should be chosen. See [how long must a Canadian Representative be appointed](/guides/how-long-must-a-canadian-representative-be-appointed/).

## How certification bodies apply it

Canadian certification bodies, and foreign certification bodies recognized under ISED's mutual recognition arrangements, are required to collect the representative's details as part of the application. In practice every CB asks for a signed letter or a completed declaration from the representative confirming its identity, Canadian address and acceptance of the section 4.1 responsibilities. Some CBs use their own template; others accept the representative's letter. Either way, the CB will not issue the certificate to a foreign applicant without it, and the product cannot be listed in ISED's Radio Equipment List (REL) or legally marketed in Canada without the certificate. See [attestation letter required fields](/guides/attestation-letter-required-fields/) and [the checklist for certification bodies](/guides/rsp-100-checklist-for-certification-bodies/).

## Who can be the representative

RSP-100 does not prescribe qualifications. Any person or entity with a genuine Canadian address who will accept the duties can serve. In practice the candidates are a Canadian subsidiary or affiliate, a Canadian distributor, a Canadian test laboratory, a consultancy, or a professional firm. The choice matters because the appointment must survive for years: a distributor relationship may end, a lab may be acquired, a consultant may retire. The comparison is set out in [representative vs certification body vs test lab](/guides/representative-vs-certification-body-vs-test-lab/) and [why a lawyer as your Canadian Representative](/guides/why-a-lawyer-as-your-canadian-representative/).

## Terminal equipment

Terminal equipment registered under DC-01 rather than certified under RSP-100 is subject to an equivalent requirement for a Canadian contact (DC-01 section 6). The practical mechanics are the same.

## Older names for the same requirement

ISED was called Industry Canada until 2015, and the certification regime has not been renamed in everyday use: labs and certification bodies still say "IC certification", products still carry an "IC" certification number (the IC ID), and the representative is still widely called the "Industry Canada representative", "IC representative" or "Canadian agent". If a certification body asks for any of these, it is asking for the RSP-100 section 4.1 Canadian Representative described on this page.

## What the requirement is not

It is not a requirement for a Canadian importer of record, which is a customs matter. It is not a requirement for a Canadian business registration. It does not make the representative the certificate holder or the applicant. And it does not transfer responsibility for compliance: the applicant remains responsible for the product meeting the applicable RSS standards.

<!--faq-->
### Does a US company certifying through a US foreign certification body still need a Canadian Representative?

Yes. The FCB's recognition under the Canada–US mutual recognition arrangement lets it issue ISED certificates, but section 4.1 applies to the applicant's address, not the CB's. US applicants need a Canadian Representative in exactly the same way as applicants anywhere else outside Canada.

### Can I name my Canadian customer as representative?

You can, if the customer has a Canadian place of business and accepts the duties in writing. Few customers do once they understand the obligation lasts for the product's life in Canada and includes producing audit samples.

### Where does the representative's information appear publicly?

The representative is recorded on the certification application held by the CB and ISED. The public Radio Equipment List shows the certificate holder and product details, not the representative.
