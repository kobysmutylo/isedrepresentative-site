## The requirement

<!--faq-->
### What is an ISED Canadian Representative?

The person or company located in Canada that a non-Canadian applicant identifies on its ISED certification application. You will also see the role called the "Industry Canada representative", "IC representative", "Canadian agent" or "local representative for Canada": ISED was named Industry Canada until 2015, certification numbers are still prefixed "IC", and many labs and engineers still use the older terms. They all mean the same appointment under RSP-100 section 4.1. RSP-100 section 4.1 makes the representative "responsible for responding to all enquiries from ISED regarding the certified product(s), including providing audit samples at no charge to ISED." Full guide: [the Canadian Representative requirement in RSP-100](/guides/canadian-representative-requirement-rsp-100/).

### Is a Canadian Representative required for "IC certification" as well as ISED certification?

Yes; they are the same thing. "IC certification", "Industry Canada certification" and "ISED certification" all refer to radio equipment certification under RSP-100, and the certification number you receive is still called an IC certification number (IC ID). The Canadian Representative requirement applies to all of them.

### Is a Canadian Representative required by ISED, or just by certification bodies?

By ISED. The requirement is written into RSP-100 section 4.1: "A Canadian representative is required when the applicant's company address is not within Canada." Certification bodies enforce it because the procedure requires them to, not as a house rule.

### Who needs a Canadian Representative?

Any applicant for ISED radio equipment certification whose company address is outside Canada, regardless of product type, and whether the certification is through a Canadian CB or a foreign certification body recognized under a mutual recognition arrangement. A Canadian subsidiary with a real Canadian address can be the applicant and avoid the requirement; a mail drop cannot.

### Does the requirement apply to terminal equipment as well as radio apparatus?

Yes. Terminal equipment registered under DC-01 is subject to an equivalent Canadian contact requirement (DC-01 section 6). Tell us if your product is terminal equipment so the letter cites the right procedure.

### Can my Canadian distributor or importer be the representative?

It can, if it is willing to accept the section 4.1 responsibilities for as long as the product is sold in Canada. Most distributors decline once they read them, because the obligation outlives the distribution agreement. See [representative vs certification body vs test lab](/guides/representative-vs-certification-body-vs-test-lab/).

### Can my test lab or certification body act as my Canadian Representative?

Some Canadian labs offer it. The questions to ask are whether the lab will still exist and still hold the appointment in eight years, what happens to the appointment if the lab is sold, and whether your product information is protected by any professional duty of confidentiality. A CB generally should not act as representative for an applicant whose product it certifies.

## The letter

<!--faq-->
### What is an attestation letter?

A signed letter from the Canadian Representative confirming its identity and Canadian address, identifying the applicant and the product, and accepting the RSP-100 section 4.1 responsibilities. The CB files it with the application. Field-by-field guide: [attestation letter required fields](/guides/attestation-letter-required-fields/).

### How quickly can I get the letter?

The same business day after payment is confirmed (Ottawa time). Card payments clear immediately; wires take one to three banking days.

### Do I need a separate letter for each product?

One letter per IC certification number. All models, HVINs and PMNs certified under that number are covered by the same letter. A new certification number needs a new letter. See [one representative letter per IC ID](/guides/one-representative-letter-per-ic-id/).

### What is a product family for this purpose?

A group of models certified under a single IC certification number, typically sharing the same radio hardware with variations in enclosure, firmware or non-radio features. If ISED and your CB accept them under one certificate, one representative letter covers them all.

### Can you revise an existing letter?

Yes: added models, changed company names, corrected addresses and new HVINs are routine. Use the [revision form](/revise/). Revisions within 30 days of issue are free; after 90 days a revision is treated as a new request at the standard fee.

### Can you sign my certification body's own representative form instead?

Yes, at no additional cost. Several CBs and FCBs prefer their own template.

## Duration and obligations

<!--faq-->
### How long does the appointment last?

Our per-certification appointment is ten years from the date of the letter. RSP-100 requires the applicant to maintain a representative "for as long as the certified product is offered on the Canadian market," so a product still on sale at year ten needs a renewal. The annual plan covers every certification filed while it is active. Full treatment: [how long must a Canadian Representative be appointed](/guides/how-long-must-a-canadian-representative-be-appointed/).

### What happens if ISED contacts you about my product?

We acknowledge, notify your designated contact the same business day, and relay your response. We do not make technical statements about the product without your instructions.

### Who provides an audit sample, and who pays?

The applicant provides it and pays for the unit and shipping; RSP-100 section 4.1 says samples are provided to ISED "at no charge to ISED," and section 12.2 puts the cost on the certificate holder. We coordinate the logistics. Guide: [ISED audit samples: who provides, who pays](/guides/ised-audit-samples-who-provides-who-pays/).

### What if I change my Canadian Representative?

You notify your CB, which updates the certification record; the new representative issues a fresh letter. Guide: [changing your Canadian Representative](/guides/changing-your-canadian-representative/).

### What if my company is acquired or renamed?

Tell us and your CB. A name change under the same legal entity is a letter revision. A transfer of the certificate to a new entity is a CB process (RSP-100 section 10 and the transfer provisions), and the new holder needs its own representative letter.
