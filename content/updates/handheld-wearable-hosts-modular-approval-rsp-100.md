Section 8 of <a href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/radio-standards-procedures-rsp/rsp-100-certification-radio-apparatus-and-broadcasting-equipment" rel="noopener">RSP-100</a> says a host product needs no certification of its own where every intentional radio emission in it comes from a certified module. Section 8.2 takes that back for one class of product. A handheld or wearable host — ISED's own examples are cellphones and smartwatches — requires host certification unless it contains only one transmitter module and that module's SAR is exempted or at or below 0.4 W/kg. Put a second module in it and host certification is required in all cases, whether or not the two ever transmit at the same time, and whatever each one's SAR figure is. For an integrator outside Canada that means a certification of its own: its own IC number, its own applicant, and its own Canadian Representative. The module makers' letters do not reach it.
</div>

## What section 8 gives you, and on what condition

RSP-100, Issue 12 of August 2019, is still the current procedure. Section 8 states the general permission in one line: "Host products alone (without modular products) are not required to be certified separately," on the condition that all the intentional radio emissions are generated by the certified modules.

Section 8.1 carries the condition integrators skip. The modules "shall be integrated into host products as per the requirements/instructions for intended use/configuration" that the module certificate holder provides. Your finished product must still meet the applicable requirements of RSS-Gen — and the RF exposure requirements of RSS-102. Those instructions are not a courtesy document. [RSS-Gen Issue 6](/updates/rss-gen-issue-6-published/) section 9.2 obliges the module certificate holder to give host manufacturers integration instructions, together with either a host label or a description of the host labelling requirements. If your supplier has sent you a certificate and nothing else, you are missing the part that actually makes the certificate usable. Ask for it.

Two kinds of module certificate exist, and the difference matters when you buy one. Modular approval, MA, means the module meets every requirement listed in Form D, the Modular Approval Attestation, which section 8.2 requires to be filed with the application. Limited modular approval, LMA, is for a module that fails one or more of those requirements. The applicant uses Form D to show how it will keep control of the final installation so the end product stays compliant. The certificate it gets back carries a condition — that module is approved for one specific host, and no other. An applicant whose module meets every MA requirement may still request LMA, provided it states the reason on the form. So an LMA certificate naming one host is not a licence to sell the module to anyone who asks. A module you sourced through a distributor may well carry exactly that restriction, and the distributor may not know it does.

## The handheld and wearable carve-out

Section 8.2 is genuinely blunt about products held in the hand or worn on the body. For handheld or wearable host devices, host certification is required, because modular certification is not adequate for them. The single exception is exactly one transmitter module in the host, and that module's SAR either exempted or below 0.4 W/kg.

Where a handheld or wearable host contains multiple modules, host certification is required in all cases. ISED says this applies whether only one module transmits at a time or several transmit simultaneously, and regardless of each module's SAR value.

Count your radios. A fitness band with one Bluetooth Low Energy module under the exposure threshold can ride on that module's certificate. Add an LTE-M radio to the same band and you have no argument left to make. The second module is the whole test. ISED does not ask whether the two ever key up together, and it does not ask you for the SAR numbers. Host certification means a test campaign on your finished product, a filing through a certification body, an IC certification number issued to you as the host applicant, and a [Radio Equipment List](/updates/reading-your-ised-rel-listing/) entry in your name.

## What it does to the representative letter

The appointment requirement in section 4.1 of RSP-100 attaches to the applicant on a certification application. Your module maker's Canadian Representative is named on the module's certification. That letter says nothing about your watch. It says nothing about the certificate ISED will issue to you for the watch either.

The Class IV permissive change route does not rescue this either. C4PC under section 10.4 covers a certified MA or LMA module put into a new host where the result changes the originally reported RF emissions characteristics or the RF exposure assessment. It is a notice filed against the module's existing certificate — and the notice has to supply the host marketing name. That is the route for a host that was entitled to rely on the module in the first place, and we covered it in [permissive changes, Class I to Class IV](/updates/permissive-changes-rsp-100-class-i-to-iv/). A handheld host that section 8.2 pushes into host certification is not amending anybody else's certificate. You are applying for your own, and the Application and Agreement that goes with it needs a Canadian Representative if your company has no address in Canada.

## What to do

- **Count the transmitter modules in anything held or worn.** Two is the line, and it bites before SAR comes into it at all. If the product is a watch, a band, a handheld reader, a headset or a phone, do this count at design stage rather than after the housing is tooled.
- **Ask your module supplier two questions.** Is the certificate MA or LMA, and may I see the Form D attestation? An LMA restricted to another manufacturer's host is genuinely cheaper to find now than after the lab has quoted you.
- **Get the integration instructions in writing.** RSS-Gen section 9.2 makes them the module holder's obligation, and section 8.1 of RSP-100 makes following them your condition of relying on the certificate.
- **Plan the label and the manual early.** RSS-Gen section 9.4.4.2 wants the host marketing name on the host label, on the packaging or in the product literature. It then wants one of two things — the module's certification number visible whenever the module is installed, or a host label reading "Contains IC: XXXXX-YYYYYYYYYYY". Section 9.3.1 wants your user manual in English and French, and it has to carry the manual requirements of every module inside the product. The [labelling requirements guide](/guides/canadian-radio-equipment-labelling-requirements/) sets out the full list.
- **If host certification is required and your company has no Canadian address,** the Canadian Representative appointment is a separate line on your certification budget, not something inherited from the module. See [the RSP-100 requirement](/guides/canadian-representative-requirement-rsp-100/) and [one letter per IC ID](/guides/one-representative-letter-per-ic-id/).

### Does a host product containing a certified module need its own ISED certification?
Usually not. RSP-100 section 8 exempts host products from separate certification where all the intentional radio emissions are generated by certified modules, and the modules are integrated according to the module certificate holder's instructions. Handheld and wearable hosts are the exception in section 8.2.

### When does a handheld or wearable host need host certification?
Whenever it contains more than one transmitter module, and whenever its single module is neither SAR-exempt nor at or below 0.4 W/kg. With multiple modules, RSP-100 section 8.2 requires host certification in all cases, regardless of simultaneous transmission or individual SAR values.

### What is the difference between MA and LMA?
Modular approval means the module meets every requirement in Form D, the Modular Approval Attestation. Limited modular approval is granted where a module fails one or more of those requirements and the applicant shows on Form D how it will control the final installation; the LMA certificate then restricts the module to a specific host.

### Does the module maker's Canadian Representative cover my host certification?
No. Under RSP-100 section 4.1 the appointment belongs to the applicant on a given certification application. A host certification is a separate application with its own applicant, so an applicant outside Canada needs its own Application and Agreement naming a representative.

### What must the host label show?
RSS-Gen section 9.4.4.2 requires the host marketing name on the host label, on the packaging or in the product literature. It also requires either the module's ISED certification number visible whenever the module is installed, or a host label beginning with "contains", "contient" or "contains/contient" — for example "Contains IC: XXXXX-YYYYYYYYYYY".
