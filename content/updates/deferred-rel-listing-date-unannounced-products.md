Section 3.4.1 of <a href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/radio-equipment-standards/radio-standards-specifications-rss/rss-gen-general-requirements-compliance-radio-apparatus" rel="noopener">RSS-Gen</a> bars any person from manufacturing, importing, distributing, leasing, offering for sale or selling a Category I radio device in Canada unless it is listed on the Radio Equipment List. One exception sits inside the same section: where ISED has approved an application for a deferred REL listing date, the device may be manufactured, imported and distributed before the listing appears. It still may not be leased, offered for sale or sold until it is. For a product you have not announced, that is the difference between stock in a Canadian warehouse on launch morning and stock at the border.
</div>

## Six verbs, and the three that come back

Section 3.4.1 governs Category I equipment — the equipment that has to be certified. The prohibition runs to six verbs, and it does not turn on your certificate. No person shall manufacture, import, distribute, lease, offer for sale or sell Category I radio apparatus in Canada unless the apparatus is listed on the REL. ISED can have granted your certification, assigned your IC number and closed the file, and section 3.4.1 still stops the shipment until the entry is actually public.

The exception gives three of the six verbs back. Where an application for a deferred REL listing date has been approved by ISED, the apparatus may be manufactured, imported and distributed in Canada. Lease, offer for sale and sell remain prohibited until the listing appears.

The line falls between supply and demand. Your contract manufacturer can build, your freight forwarder can clear customs, your Canadian distributor can take delivery and hold the pallets. What nobody can do is move the product to a buyer. A price list, a reseller quote, a live pre-order page — each of those is probably an offer for sale on its face, and none of them waits for a deferral the way a warehouse does.

## What the deferral actually withholds

RSP-100 explains the purpose in one line that is easy to miss. Section 9.1 describes the key fields in a certification application, and of the Product Marketing Name it says two things: the PMN is required at the time of the certification, and for temporary non-disclosure of the PMN, the deferred REL/TAR listing function can be used.

So ISED is not keeping your product from ISED. The PMN goes in with the application like every other field. What the function withholds is publication — the moment your marketing name becomes searchable by anyone with a browser, which under the default rule is exactly the moment you become allowed to ship.

That matters because of who reads the <a href="https://sms-sgs.ic.gc.ca/equipmentSearch/searchRadioEquipments?execution=e1s1&lang=en_CA" rel="noopener">Radio Equipment List</a>. Competitive analysts and trade journalists watch it the way they watch the FCC's database, and a new entry under a known company number is a dated, official signal that a product exists. We set out what an entry exposes in [how to read your REL listing](/updates/reading-your-ised-rel-listing/): the company number and name, the IC number, the approval date, the HVIN, PMN, HMN and FVIN, the equipment description, your certification body and test lab, and an emissions table. For an unannounced product, the PMN and the equipment description are the two fields that tell a reader what you built.

## Four things the deferral does not do

**It does not delay certification.** The deferral attaches to the listing date, not to the grant. Your application, your test report and your fees run on the ordinary schedule, and the deferral is useless to anyone who has not already finished.

**It does not protect your exhibits.** Confidentiality of the filing is a separate regime in RSP-100 section 12.4, where the applicant has to indicate which documents are confidential. Block diagrams, operational descriptions, parts lists, software-defined radio and security information, and schematics qualify for confidential treatment; other exhibit types may be treated as confidential on a justified request made before you submit. Section 12.4 also states plainly that the *Access to Information Act* applies to all applicants, and we work through what that means when a request lands in [access to information requests for certification files](/guides/access-to-information-requests-certification-filings/). A deferred listing date buys you a quiet database entry. It does nothing at all about a section 20 notice.

**It does not let you sell early.** Three verbs stay prohibited for the whole deferral, and the prohibition in section 3.4.1 runs against any person — your distributor and your retailer are inside it, not only you.

**It is not the research-and-development exemption.** Section 3.9 exempts apparatus used solely for research and development, experimentation, demonstration or assessing marketability from certification and labelling altogether, and may require a developmental licence from your ISED District Office. That route also forbids lease, sale and offering for sale. A deferral is for a product you have certified and intend to sell on a date you have chosen; section 3.9 is for a product you are not selling at all.

## Where your representative sits in this

Section 4.1 of RSP-100 requires a Canadian representative when the applicant's company address is not within Canada, and the representative is identified in the signed Application and Agreement for Certification Services — a document that goes in with the application. The appointment is therefore in place well before certification, and long before any listing date, deferred or not. Nothing about a deferral changes the requirement, the timing or the letter.

It cuts the other way too. The representative answers ISED's enquiries about the certified product and provides audit samples at no charge, and those obligations are live from certification, which is to say during the deferral period, when the product is in Canada and the listing is not yet public. An ISED enquiry can arrive about a device your own press release has not mentioned. The [RSP-100 representative requirement](/guides/canadian-representative-requirement-rsp-100/) guide sets out the full scope.

## What ISED does not publish

Neither RSS-Gen nor RSP-100 states how long a listing may be deferred, what the application for a deferred listing date has to contain, or what ISED weighs in approving one. Section 3.4.1 says only that the application must have been approved; section 9.1 says simply that the function exists. There is no published maximum. We would not quote one.

Treat the length as something to settle with your certification body before you commit to a launch date, in writing, at the same time you settle the testing schedule. Your CB files the application. It will know what ISED has recently approved. Ask early: a deferral you assumed and did not request is indistinguishable, at the border, from no deferral at all.

## What to do

- **Ask for the deferral when you file, not when you ship.** The approval has to exist before the manufacturing, importing and distributing permission in section 3.4.1 does any work for you.
- **Get the deferral length in writing from your certification body.** ISED's published documents set no maximum, so the only reliable number is the one your CB confirms for your file.
- **Keep the commercial side dark until the listing appears.** Pre-order pages, distributor price lists and reseller quotes are the exposure, and the prohibition binds your channel as well as you.
- **Deal with confidentiality separately, before submission.** Section 12.4 is where your schematics and operational description are protected, and the justified-request route closes once you file. See the [access to information guide](/guides/access-to-information-requests-certification-filings/).
- **Check your own entry on the listing date.** If the deferral has lapsed or the PMN published early, you want to know before a journalist does. The [IC number lookup guide](/guides/ised-certification-number-lookup-rel/) shows where to search.
- **Have the representative appointed before you file.** Section 4.1 puts the name in the Application and Agreement, so this is a step at the front of the process, not the end.

<!--faq-->
### What is a deferred REL listing date?
It is an ISED-approved postponement of the date your certified product appears on the public Radio Equipment List. RSP-100 section 9.1 describes its purpose as temporary non-disclosure of the Product Marketing Name. The PMN is still required at the time of certification; the deferral affects publication, not what you file.

### Can I import into Canada before my product is listed on the REL?
Only with an approved deferred REL listing date. Section 3.4.1 of RSS-Gen otherwise prohibits manufacturing, importing, distributing, leasing, offering for sale and selling Category I apparatus until it is listed. Where the deferral is approved, manufacturing, importing and distributing are permitted; the product still may not be leased, offered for sale or sold until the listing appears.

### How long can an ISED listing be deferred?
ISED's published documents do not state a maximum. RSS-Gen section 3.4.1 requires only that the application have been approved, and RSP-100 section 9.1 notes that the function exists. Confirm the period with the certification body that files your application.

### Does a deferred listing keep my test report and schematics confidential?
No. Confidentiality of the filing is governed by RSP-100 section 12.4, where you identify which documents are confidential when you apply, and by the *Access to Information Act*. A deferred listing date postpones a public database entry and nothing else.

### Does a deferral change when I need a Canadian Representative?
No. Section 4.1 of RSP-100 requires the representative where the applicant's company address is outside Canada, and names that representative in the signed Application and Agreement filed with the application. The appointment precedes certification and any listing date.
