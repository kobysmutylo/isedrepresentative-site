Alongside RSS-Gen Issue 6, ISED published <a href="https://ised-isde.canada.ca/site/spectrum-management-telecommunications/en/devices-and-equipment/radio-equipment-standards/radio-standards-specifications-rss/rss-310-licence-exempt-radio-apparatus-category-ii-equipment" rel="noopener">RSS-310 Issue 6</a> on July 30, 2026 — the standard for licence-exempt <em>Category II</em> equipment, which is exempt from certification. The practical consequence for foreign manufacturers: Category II devices don't go through a certification application at all, so no Canadian Representative letter arises for them. The representative requirement continues to apply where it always has — Category I equipment certified under RSP-100 by an applicant located outside Canada.

## What changed in Issue 6

Stand-alone receivers, previously handled in RSS-Gen, now sit in RSS-310, with their own treatment — under RSS-310 they are exempt from labelling and test-report requirements. Requirements for passive RFID tags also moved into RSS-310 from RSS-Gen. The revision removes sections that duplicated RSS-Gen, simplifies the labelling and manual provisions, and replaces the old blanket exemptions for extremely low-power transmitters with targeted exemptions from specific documentation requirements. As with RSS-Gen, a one-year transition applies: compliance with either Issue 5 or Issue 6 is accepted until the transition ends.

## Category I vs Category II — the distinction that decides everything

Canadian device compliance runs on this split. **Category I** equipment requires certification: an application through a certification body, an IC certification number, listing in the Radio Equipment List — and, when the applicant's address is outside Canada, a [Canadian Representative under RSP-100 section 4.1](/guides/canadian-representative-requirement-rsp-100/). **Category II** equipment must still comply with the applicable technical standard, and the manufacturer keeps records demonstrating that compliance — but there is no certification application, and therefore no representative letter in that pathway.

Which category a product falls into is determined by the applicable RSS standard, not by guesswork — your testing laboratory or certification body confirms it. Many products that people assume are exempt (Wi-Fi, Bluetooth, most intentional transmitters) are Category I.

## What to actually do

If your product line mixes transmitters and receive-only devices, the RSS-310 revision is a good prompt to confirm each product's category with your lab before budgeting for certification. For the Category I products, the certification file will need the representative letter — [US$499 per certification or US$999/year unlimited](/pricing/), issued the same business day.

<!--faq-->
### Does a Category II device need a Canadian Representative?
No. Category II equipment is exempt from certification, so there is no certification application and no representative letter in that pathway. The requirement applies to Category I certification applicants located outside Canada.

### Are stand-alone receivers now exempt from labelling in Canada?
Under RSS-310 Issue 6, stand-alone receivers subject to RSS-310 are exempt from labelling and test-report requirements. They must still comply with the standard itself — confirm the details for your product with your testing laboratory.

### My product transmits and receives. Does RSS-310 apply?
Generally no — transceivers are governed by the product-specific RSS standard for their radio technology and are typically Category I, requiring certification. RSS-310's receiver provisions concern stand-alone (receive-only) apparatus.
