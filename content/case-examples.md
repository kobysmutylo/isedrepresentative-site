## The letter the CB is waiting for

A Taiwanese ODM's certification body had completed its review of a Wi-Fi 6 router family and was holding the certificate for one missing item: the Canadian Representative declaration. The ODM's US brand customer had a launch date in ten days. The order arrived at 21:00 Taipei time; the letter, on the CB's own template, went back before the Ottawa business day ended, and the certificate issued the following day. What made it fast was not urgency but preparation: the ODM supplied the IC number, PMN and HVIN list exactly as they appeared on the application, so nothing needed to be reconciled. See [attestation letter required fields](/guides/attestation-letter-required-fields/).

## The audit sample request

Four years into an appointment for an industrial LoRaWAN gateway, ISED requested a post-certification audit sample. The European manufacturer had changed its regulatory contact twice since certification and the original contact's mailbox bounced. Because we keep a secondary contact and the manufacturer's general regulatory address on file, we reached the right person the same day, relayed ISED's shipping instructions, and confirmed dispatch within the week. The episode is why we ask for two contacts and why we remind clients annually to confirm them. See [ISED audit samples](/guides/ised-audit-samples-who-provides-who-pays/).

## The rename that was really a transfer

A US startup certified a Bluetooth wearable, then asked us to "update the company name" on the letter. The new name belonged to the acquiring company, a different legal entity. A letter revision would have been wrong: the certificate holder had changed, which is a CB transfer process, and the new holder needed its own appointment. We explained the distinction, the CB processed the transfer, and we issued a fresh letter to the acquirer. See [changing your Canadian Representative](/guides/changing-your-canadian-representative/).

## The module maker and its forty host integrators

A South Korean RF module manufacturer held one certification covering a module used by dozens of host-product integrators. Several integrators assumed they needed their own Canadian Representative letter for the module. They did not: the module certification belongs to the module maker, and one representative letter covers it. Integrators need a letter only where they hold their own certification for the host or for a Class II permissive change. See [one representative letter per IC ID](/guides/one-representative-letter-per-ic-id/).

## The lab on an annual plan

A European test laboratory with a steady flow of Asian and US clients moved from ordering letters one at a time to an annual plan. Its project managers now submit the order form directly, letters issue the same day, and the lab invoices its clients as part of the project. The representative named is still us; the applicant is always the lab's client. See [for labs and certification bodies](/for-labs-and-certification-bodies/).

## The product still on sale at year ten

A smart-metering client's first appointment with us reached the end of its ten-year term while the meter family remained in service and on the Canadian market. We contacted the client three months ahead of expiry, confirmed the certification was still active, and renewed at the per-certification fee. Nothing changed on the ISED record, since the representative was the same. See [how long must a Canadian Representative be appointed](/guides/how-long-must-a-canadian-representative-be-appointed/).
